# Sales enablement reference

Assets reps actually use: specific, scannable, defensible. Every claim needs a source the rep can cite on a call; one strong case study beats ten weak logos.

Contents: 1 pitch deck · 2 one-pager · 3 objection library · 4 live objection help · 5 battle cards · 6 win-loss · 7 ROI case · 8 discovery script · 9 demo script · 10 cold call script · 11 case study brief · 12 proposal · 13 playbook · 14 persona cards · 15 asset audit.

Start by confirming: what's being sold and to whom (ICP, personas, deal size, cycle), which stage the asset serves (prospecting, discovery, demo, negotiation, close), who uses it (new reps, experienced AEs, champions forwarding internally), and what proof exists.

## 1. Pitch deck (10-12 slides)

1. **Problem**: the pain in the buyer's words.
2. **Cost of the problem**: time, money, risk; quantified when possible.
3. **The shift**: what changed that makes a new approach possible or necessary.
4. **Your approach**: how you solve it (concept, not features).
5. **Walkthrough**: 3-4 key capabilities tied to the problem.
6. **Proof**: logos, metrics, quotes from similar customers.
7. **Case study**: one customer, before → after with numbers.
8. **Implementation**: timeline, effort, what's needed from them.
9. **ROI**: their numbers where possible.
10. **Pricing**: options or the range (if appropriate for the stage).
11. **Next steps**: specific, dated, mutual.

Customize by buyer: technical (architecture, security, integrations, implementation detail), economic (ROI, risk, strategic fit, peers), champion (materials they can forward internally, objection answers, a short internal pitch). Keep ≤ 30 words per slide, one idea per slide, speaker notes for depth.

## 2. One-pager

Headline (outcome) → problem (2-3 bullets) → solution (3 capabilities with outcomes) → proof (metric + logo/quote) → how it works / getting started → contact + clear next step. One page, skimmable in 30 seconds; a version per persona if priorities differ; written to be forwarded without you in the room.

## 3. Objection library

Categories: price/budget, timing, competition, authority (not the decision-maker), status quo ("we're fine"), technical/security, risk ("too new"), effort ("no time to implement").

Format per objection: the objection as said → why they really say it (underlying concern) → response (acknowledge, reframe, answer) → proof point → follow-up question to keep the conversation going.

Example: "Too expensive" → really: value unclear vs. alternatives, or no budget this cycle → "Fair; most teams compare us to doing it manually. Your team spends ~30 hours a month on receipts; at loaded cost that's ~$1,500/month against $129." → case study with payback → "What would it need to save for it to be a clear yes?"

Rules: acknowledge before answering; never argue; one strong proof beats a list; if it's a real gap, say so and move to fit; log new objections to refresh the library monthly.

## 4. Live objection help

When a rep pastes a live objection: identify the category and the likely underlying concern (ask one clarifying question if ambiguous) → give a 2-3 sentence response the rep can say naturally → one proof point → one question to ask next → note what to avoid saying. Pull from the library first; flag new objections for it.

## 5. Battle cards

One per competitor; named competitor required; reviewed quarterly or after a major competitor change. Template: [templates/battle-card.md](templates/battle-card.md).

Sections: when you'll see them (segments, deal types) · why buyers consider them (their honest strengths) · where they're strong (concede it) · where we win (with proof) · discovery questions that surface our strengths ("How do you handle X today?") · their common claims and our responses · landmines they set about us and how to defuse them · pricing comparison (as of a date) · win/loss notes · when to walk away (deals where they're a better fit) · last verified date and sources.

Never trash the competitor; reps lose credibility. Accuracy matters more than spin; a buyer who catches one false claim discounts everything.

## 6. Win-loss

Interview 15-30 minutes, within 2-4 weeks of the decision, ideally by someone other than the rep. Cover: trigger for the search, alternatives considered, decision criteria and weights, who was involved, moments that tipped it, what we could have done better, perception of price/value. Code reasons (product gap, price, relationship, timing, competitor strength, process) and review patterns quarterly; feed back to battle cards, objection library and product.

## 7. ROI case

Inputs from the buyer: team size, hours on the task, loaded hourly cost, error/rework rate, revenue impact. Calculation: current cost of the problem − cost with the product − product cost = net value; payback months = upfront + annual cost ÷ monthly savings. Show conservative/expected/aggressive; label every assumption and source; let the buyer change inputs; avoid inflated "10× ROI" claims that champions can't defend internally.

## 8. Discovery script

Opening: agenda and purpose, confirm time, ask for permission to ask questions. Then: situation (current process, tools, team) → problem (what's hard, how often, how bad) → impact (cost, who's affected, what happens if nothing changes) → priority and timing (why now, deadlines) → decision process (who's involved, criteria, past purchases, budget) → next step (agree to something specific and dated). Talk ≤ 40% of the time; summarize back; capture the buyer's words for later use.

## 9. Demo script

Personalized to discovery notes; show the 2-3 things that solve their stated problems, not every feature.

| Scene | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Recap | 2-3 min | Confirm what you heard in discovery; ask what's changed |
| Context | 1-2 min | The "before" in their terms |
| Scene 1-3 | 5-8 min each | Problem → show the fix → value in their numbers → check reaction |
| Proof | 2 min | Similar customer's result |
| Questions | 5-10 min | Invite objections |
| Next steps | 3-5 min | Specific, dated, mutual plan |

Prepare a fallback if something breaks; use their data or realistic data; stop and ask questions after each scene.

## 10. Cold call script

Permission opener → reason for the call tied to a signal (one sentence) → relevant proof → interest question → handle the brush-off once ("Totally fair; is it that it's not a priority, or that you've got it covered?") → ask for a short meeting or send a note. Have 3-5 openers by persona and a voicemail version (cold-email.md §10).

## 11. Case study brief

Customer profile (industry, size, role of champion) → situation before (with numbers) → why they chose you (alternatives considered) → implementation (time, effort) → results (metrics with timeframes) → quote → what's next. Get approval for names and numbers; produce versions: one-paragraph, one-pager, full story, slide.

## 12. Proposal

Executive summary in their words (problem, outcome, investment) → understanding of their situation → proposed solution and scope → implementation plan and timeline → success metrics → pricing options (good/better/best where fitting) → terms → next steps and signature path. Short beats long; mirror their decision criteria.

## 13. Playbook

For a segment or motion: ICP and personas → qualification criteria (and disqualifiers) → stage-by-stage plays with exit criteria → talk tracks and assets per stage → objection handling → competitive guidance → tools and process → metrics to watch. Living document with an owner.

## 14. Persona cards

Per persona: role and reports-to, goals and metrics they're measured on, pains, what they care about in a purchase, common objections, how they buy (research habits, influences), language they use, the message that works, proof that resonates, what to avoid.

## 15. Asset audit

Inventory existing assets → map to stage and persona → mark stale (> 6 months without review), unused (ask reps and check usage data), missing (stages with no asset) → prioritize the gaps that block deals most often.
