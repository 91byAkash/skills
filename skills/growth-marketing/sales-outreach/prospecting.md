# Prospecting reference

Contents: 1 branches · 2 ICP · 3 five phases · 4 signals, freshness and plays · 5 tiers and account research · 6 demand-signal branch · 7 local SMB branch · 8 compliance · 9 data sources by job · 10 lead sheet formats.

## 1. Branches

| Branch | Sell to | Qualified means | Typical sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| SaaS | Software and digital businesses | ICP fit + tech-stack match + growth signals (funding, hiring, product velocity) | Professional network, tech-detection tools, funding databases, launch sites, licensed contact data |
| B2B | Services firms, manufacturers, mid-market and enterprise | Industry + size + geography + trigger events (vendor change, expansion) | Licensed B2B databases, professional network, industry directories, trade associations |
| Local SMB | Shops, gyms, clinics, salons, trades | Active business + website status + proximity + owner reachable | Map listings, review sites, local directories, the business's own site and social pages |
| Demand-signal | First customers, design partners, beta users | Public evidence of the exact pain, demand or timing | Forums, review sites, issue trackers, public posts, Q&A sites |

## 2. ICP

One paragraph plus a pass/fail checklist covering: firmographics (industry, size, revenue band, geography, model), technographics (tools used or missing), buying signal (why now), decision-maker (role, seniority, priorities), disqualifiers (clear skips). Pull from the context file where possible; derive from best existing customers (shortest cycle, highest retention, best expansion).

## 3. Five phases

1. **Define ICP** (above).
2. **Discover**: source 2-3× the final target; combine 2-3 sources to cross-verify; quality over size (25 verified leads beat 250 mostly junk).
3. **Qualify**: score against the checklist with a source URL per criterion. Confidence: High = 2+ independent sources or official page; Medium = one credible source + consistent evidence; Low = incomplete or ambiguous (flag what's uncertain). Verify email deliverability before listing; re-verify within 7 days of sending; set a policy for catch-all domains (e.g., send only to Tier 1 catch-alls with strong signals).
4. **Score and prioritize**: Hot = strong fit + clear signal + reachable decision-maker + verified contact; Warm = fit + softer/older signal + verifiable contact; Cold = loose fit or no signal or unverified; Skip = disqualifier, closed, duplicate, low confidence. Typical mix ~20% Hot, ~30% Warm. Then tier (§5).
5. **Output the lead sheet** (§10) followed by "top outreach targets": 3-5 Hot leads with one sentence each on why first.

## 4. Signals, freshness and plays

The closer a signal is to your own product, the more it is worth. Strongest first, each with how long it stays fresh and the first move:

1. **They use your product** (sign-up from a company domain, plan limit reached, large import, teammates invited). They have already picked you once. Fresh for hours: respond the same day, ideally within minutes; after that, treat it as an ordinary product-qualified lead. First move: a short offer of help growing past the current plan, from the founder or account owner by email, followed by LinkedIn.
2. **A former champion starts a new job.** People bring familiar tools with them, and deals with a buyer who already knows the product close far more often. Best 30-90 days into the role; later, their stack is settled. First move: recall the earlier work together and ask how the new team handles the same job (LinkedIn, then email).
3. **High-intent site visits** (pricing, comparison or integration pages), only where identifying the visitor is lawful. They are evaluating now; act within 24 hours, then fold them into general lists. First move: never mention the visit; speak to the choice they are probably weighing (email; add a call for Tier 1).
4. **Public engagement** with you or a competitor (comments, community questions). Shows interest in the problem. First move: talk about the problem they raised, not the competitor (LinkedIn, then email).
5. **Hiring for the function you serve, or a new leader in it.** New money and a mandate. A job post is live for up to ~60 days and goes cold once the hire is made; a new leader sets priorities in the first 90 days. First move: what can be done before the hire starts (or what the new hire will need), by email; for new leaders, the problem people in that role usually tackle first, with proof (LinkedIn, then email).
6. **Funding, launches or expansion.** Budget plus pressure to grow; useful for 30-60 days, after which it is old news (and skip the congratulations).
7. **A tool next to yours is installed or removed.** A related decision is in motion; about 30 days before it is settled.
8. **Generic third-party intent feeds.** The weakest and noisiest; use only to break ties.

## 5. Tiers and account research

- **Tier 1, one-to-one**: your best 25-50 accounts. Spend 10-20 minutes on each (or an agent run that a person reviews) and write a full brief; every message is written for that person; use email, LinkedIn, phone and video, and send physical mail only once they have engaged.
- **Tier 2, one-to-few**: segments of 20-200 accounts with the same pain. A shared segment brief plus 2-3 minutes per contact to find one sourced hook; segment-level copy carrying that hook; email and LinkedIn, phone only for the strongest fits.
- **Tier 3, one-to-many**: the rest of the ICP. Segment brief only, segment copy, email only.

Where useful facts live: job posts (pain in their words, tools, priorities); their own pages (pricing, landing pages, changelog, careers, case studies; for services, the page you'd improve is the research); leaders' public statements (posts, podcasts, talks); news (funding, launches, acquisitions, leadership changes, new markets); public filings (risk factors and earnings calls name priorities); reviews of their product (what they're under pressure to fix).

Tier 1 brief: company snapshot; why now (signal + date + source); likely priorities; people (decision-maker, champion candidates, their public statements); our best angle mapped to one of our 3-5 value propositions; proof that fits; risks/disqualifiers; first-touch hook. Template: [templates/account-brief.md](templates/account-brief.md).

Research rules (especially for agents): facts first (each with URL and date), angle second using only those facts; "no hook found" is valid; bound angles to your value propositions; date everything and discard stale facts; quote exactly when a hook rests on someone's words; never use sensitive traits.

## 6. Demand-signal branch (first customers)

Different from list building: starts from a described problem, sources public discourse, contacts people where they posted, wins on 10 strong evidence-backed matches, outputs an evidence report and manual outreach plan.

1. Product brief first: problem, who has it, current workarounds, what "solved" looks like, what you can offer now.
2. Mine five buckets: explicit demand ("looking for", "alternative to", "does anything exist"), pain ("takes hours", "so manual", "keeps breaking"), workaround (spreadsheets, copy-paste, assistants, glue scripts), switching (cancelling, migrating, pricing complaints), timing (launch, new hire, regulation, integration announcement).
3. Public sources only; respect community rules (many forbid unsolicited DMs or promotion).
4. Score demand-fit 0-100: pain strength 25%, product fit 25%, timing 20%, public reachability 15%, evidence quality 15%. 80-100 strong candidate; 65-79 validate fast; 50-64 missing a signal; < 50 exclude.
5. Evidence ledger per prospect: quote, link, date, bucket, why it fits, suggested approach.
6. Draft outreach for the user to send manually, replying in context where appropriate; never mass-message.

## 7. Local SMB branch

Website status is the core axis for web/marketing services: no site found → Hot; social-only (social pages, booking portal, link page, marketplace page) → Hot; weak site (outdated, broken, thin, not mobile-friendly, no clear contact) → Warm; good modern site → low unless other gaps (poor search visibility, weak conversion design).

Other signals: operational (open hours current, recent reviews, photos, owner replies), proximity (within target radius), decay (closed, moved, inactive listing).

Workflow: map search for category + area → open each listing → check website, social, phone, reviews → cross-check on review sites/directories → record status with evidence. Score: Hot = no site/social-only + phone + active + in radius; Warm = weak site or marketplace-only; Cold = good site or low confidence; Skip = closed, duplicate, out of radius, irrelevant.

Top targets: no site + phone present; high review counts (established); owners who reply to reviews (engaged); category matching your specialty.

## 8. Compliance

Guardrails for every branch:
1. No bulk scraping of professional networks, map listings, paywalled or rate-limited sites; assisted browsing only. Programmatic extraction is acceptable only from individual prospects' own websites, within their terms.
2. No CAPTCHA, login-wall or bot-protection bypass.
3. Public business contacts (info@, named-role addresses on the business's site) or licensed data; avoid personal addresses.
4. Record source URL and collection date for every contact (needed for lawful-basis and opt-out handling).
5. Don't resell data from platforms whose terms prohibit it.
6. Rate-limit requests.
7. No breached, leaked or provenance-free data; no list brokers without lineage.
8. Never target, infer or personalize on sensitive traits (health, finances, politics, religion, sexuality, family).

Regional notes (not legal advice): US commercial email requires accurate headers, no deceptive subjects, a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, and a postal address. EU/UK B2B cold outreach usually relies on legitimate interest: document the assessment, keep it relevant to the role, offer easy opt-out, minimize data and retention, honor deletion requests; UK electronic marketing rules distinguish corporate and individual subscribers. Canada requires consent (implied consent covers some published business addresses when the message relates to their role). Australia requires consent (inferred consent possible for conspicuously published business addresses) and functional unsubscribe. US phone/SMS: scrub do-not-call lists, respect calling hours, never use automated or AI voice to mobiles without prior consent.

Audit checklist: sources recorded; lawful basis documented; verification done; opt-out line and address in every email; suppression list shared across channels; retention policy set.

## 9. Data sources by job

| Job | Source types |
|---|---|
| Firmographics + contacts | Licensed B2B contact databases |
| Multi-source enrichment and waterfall email/phone lookup | Enrichment platforms and aggregators |
| Hiring and tech-stack signals | Job-post aggregators, tech-detection tools |
| Funding | Funding databases, press releases |
| Decision-maker mapping | Professional network search (manual) |
| Email verification | Verification services (before every upload) |
| Dev-tool audiences | Public code-hosting activity on competitor or adjacent projects |
| Local businesses | Map listings + browser |
| No tools | Company site, about page, company network page, news; slower but works |

## 10. Lead sheet formats

SaaS/B2B table (≤ 25 rows): score · company · industry · size · signal · contact · email status · source · confidence.
Local SMB table (≤ 15 rows): score · business · category · area · website status · website/social · phone · why it's a prospect · confidence.
CSV (> 25 rows or on request): score, company, domain, industry, size_band, country, signal, signal_date, contact_name, contact_title, contact_email, email_status, profile_url, source_urls, why_prospect, confidence, verified_date, tier, notes.
