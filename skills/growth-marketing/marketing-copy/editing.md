# Editing and refresh reference

Contents: 1 principles · 2 seven sweeps · 3 reviewer-panel scoring · 4 quick checks · 5 plain-English swaps · 6 content refresh · 7 edit checklist.

## 1. Principles

Keep the core message; improve how it lands. Several focused passes beat one unfocused read. Every edit needs a reason. Preserve the author's voice. Never add claims you can't support; flag them.

## 2. Seven sweeps

Run in order. After each sweep, re-check the earlier ones briefly (edits in later sweeps can break clarity or voice).

| Sweep | Question | Look for | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Clarity | Can a newcomer understand every sentence on first read? | Overloaded sentences, unclear pronouns, jargon, buried points, missing context | Split, define, lead with the point; one idea per section; speak to "you" |
| 2 Voice and tone | Does it sound like one brand throughout? | Casual-to-corporate drift, "we" vs "the company", random technical register, misplaced humor | Read aloud; smooth shifts to the defined voice |
| 3 So what | Does every claim answer "why should I care?" | Features without benefits, company achievements that don't help the reader | Add a "which means…" bridge to a real outcome |
| 4 Prove it | Is each claim supported? | "Trusted by thousands", "industry-leading", outcomes without numbers | Add specific proof nearby, soften, or flag `[NEED: proof]` |
| 5 Specificity | Is it concrete? | improve/enhance/optimize, round made-up numbers, outcomes without timeframes | Numbers, timeframes, examples; cut what can't be made specific |
| 6 Heightened emotion | Does the reader feel the pain and the relief? | Flat informational tone, abstract pains | Concrete moments, sensory detail, the reader's words; keep it truthful |
| 7 Zero risk | Is every barrier near the CTA removed? | Unanswered objections, missing trust signals, vague next steps, surprise costs, scary fine print | Guarantee, trial terms, "no card", privacy note, what happens next, proof next to the CTA |

Specificity upgrades: "save time" → "save 4 hours a week"; "many customers" → "2,847 teams"; "fast results" → "results in 14 days"; "great support" → "replies within 2 hours".

## 3. Reviewer-panel scoring

For launch copy, pricing pages and high-traffic pages (recommended for sales pages, email sequences and ads; skip for minor edits): review through 3-5 lenses, each scoring 1-10 with specific critiques; fix the lowest first; re-score until every lens ≥ 7 and average ≥ 8.

| Copy type | Lenses |
|---|---|
| Landing page | Conversion copywriter (clarity, CTA, benefit order) · UX writer (scannability, cognitive load) · target customer (speaks to me? trust it?) · brand strategist (voice, positioning) |
| Email sequence | Email specialist (subject lines, clicks) · copywriter (hooks, story) · deliverability reviewer (spam triggers) · target customer (relevance, unsubscribe risk) |
| Long-form sales page | Direct-response copywriter (offer, objections, urgency) · skeptical buyer (proof gaps, red flags) · editor (flow, concision) · search specialist (intent, coverage) |

Scale: 9-10 publish; 7-8 minor tweaks; 5-6 clear gaps; 3-4 major revision; 1-2 rethink.

## 4. Quick checks

Words: cut weak intensifiers (very, really, extremely, incredibly), filler (just, actually, basically), "in order to" → "to", unnecessary "that"; replace buzzwords with facts; remove exclamation points.

Sentences: one idea each; front-load the point; under ~20 words on average with variety; active voice; no stacked qualifiers; parallel lists; no sentences starting with "There is/are" unless needed.

Paragraphs: 1-3 sentences on the web; first sentence carries the point; subheads every 3-4 paragraphs in long copy that make a claim.

Formatting: scannable headings, bullets for parallel items only, bold sparingly, numbers as digits, sentence case.

## 6. Content refresh

**When**: traffic falling on a once-strong page; stats older than 12 months; product, pricing or positioning changed; competitors updated their version; the page is losing citations in AI answers (see ai-search-optimization).

**Find the gap first**
1. Read the current results page for the main query: dominant format (list, guide, tool, comparison), snippet format, related questions, related searches.
2. Pick five comparison pages: top three results plus any direct competitor or recently updated page near you.
3. Grid subtopics/elements (rows) × your page and the five (columns).
4. Classify gaps: expected (4-5 of 5 cover it → add), common (2-3 → add if it serves the reader), distinctive (1 → only if genuinely useful), uncovered (0 → your chance to be the page worth citing).
5. Match coverage, not word count; stop when the question is answered.

Present the grid, a Keep/Revise/New outline and 2-3 title options before rewriting (title patterns in seo-optimization).

**Passes**: freshness (dates, stats, examples, deprecated tools) → accuracy (claims, links, pricing, features) → voice → search (intent shifts, new questions, visible "Last updated") → proof (newer testimonials, data) → structure (tables, FAQ, scannable sections).

**Protect what works**: keep the URL (301 if it must change), heading anchors others link to, sections that rank for their own queries (check the page's query report), and the voice. Put the year next to every statistic.

**Hand-off notes**: new meta description, schema to add, internal links to and from, images/screenshots, URL plan.

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Message valid, details stale | Refresh |
| Voice has evolved | Refresh + voice pass |
| Angle or audience shifted | Full rewrite |
| Structure doesn't match intent | Full rewrite |
| Only stats and links stale | Light refresh |

Cadence: pricing/product pages quarterly or on change; high-traffic posts every 6 months; evergreen guides yearly; comparison pages quarterly.

## 7. Edit checklist

- [ ] Goal, audience and desired action understood; read once without editing
- [ ] Clarity: every sentence understood first time; jargon defined
- [ ] Voice: consistent, reads well aloud
- [ ] So what: each feature tied to a benefit
- [ ] Prove it: claims supported, proof attributed, no unearned superlatives
- [ ] Specificity: numbers, timeframes, examples
- [ ] Emotion: pains and aspirations feel real and true
- [ ] Zero risk: objections, trust signals and next steps near CTAs
- [ ] Machine-sounding patterns removed (see ai-tells.md); lint run
- [ ] Every change has a stated reason
