# Offer construction reference

The offer is the bundle the buyer evaluates: what they get, what else, what if it fails, why now, what it's called, and how they pay. A strong offer with average copy beats a weak offer with great copy.

## 1. Value equation

Perceived value rises with **dream outcome** and **perceived likelihood of achieving it**, and falls with **time to result** and **effort and sacrifice**. Price is what value is compared against; most "we must lower the price" requests are really "raise the top or shrink the bottom" requests.

| Lever | Score it (1-10) by asking | Ways to improve |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome ↑ | Can the buyer picture the day after it works, and repeat the outcome to a friend? | Name the bigger goal behind the surface ask ("more leads" → "a full pipeline without founder-led sales"); make it specific and measurable |
| Likelihood ↑ | Are there 3+ comparable named proof points? Is the method specific? Is "my situation is different" answered? | Case studies from look-alike buyers, named methodology, data, demos, guarantee, credentials that matter |
| Time to result ↓ | When is the first concrete win? End-to-end timeline? Any onboarding lag? | Quick-start, done-with-you setup, first-win in week 1, templates, faster delivery |
| Effort/sacrifice ↓ | How much work, decisions, learning, live time, social risk? | Done-for-you pieces, fewer choices, defaults, async options, migration help |

Diagnose: score all four; the lowest is the binding constraint. Fix one lever per iteration (the easiest of the lowest if tied), relaunch, measure, then the next. Honest expectation: a single component change often moves conversion something like 10-40%; two changes on different levers can compound further; claims of 5× are a red flag.

## 2. The six components

| # | Component | Buyer question | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core deliverable | What do I get? | Feature list instead of outcome; fuzzy scope |
| 2 | Bonuses | What else makes this a no-brainer? | None, or generic padding |
| 3 | Guarantee | What if it doesn't work? | None, wrong type, or over-promise |
| 4 | Scarcity/urgency | Why now? | None, or fake |
| 5 | Name | What is it called? | Generic or internal jargon |
| 6 | Price + payment | What do I pay and how? | One structure only; price shown without comparison |

Core deliverable: define as an outcome with explicit scope (what's in, out, optional), timeline and format. Match depth to awareness: unaware buyers need the problem framed; most-aware buyers need terms and the deal.

## 3. Guarantees

| Type | Promise | Works for | Backfires when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unconditional refund | Money back within N days, no questions | Low-price info products, high-trust audiences | High-touch or high-price delivery (refunds eat margin) |
| Conditional refund | Refund if you did X and didn't get Y | Courses/programs that require effort | Too many conditions (reads as a trap) |
| Better-than-refund | Refund plus extra | Confident delivery, ample margin | The few failures become public stories |
| Service level | If we miss X by date Y, you don't pay | Productized services, agencies | SLA isn't measurable |
| Performance / pay-on-results | Pay only when X happens | Sophisticated B2B, measurable outcomes | Long cycles, attribution disputes |
| Anti-guarantee | No refunds; qualify first | Premium, application-based, mature buyers | First-time buyers (reads cold or scammy) |
| Outcome-or-extension | Not there by Y? We keep working free | Coaching, services with extendable time | Open-ended cost |
| Comparison | Beat your current result/tool or refund | When the comparison is cleanly measurable | Can't measure the baseline |

Choose by: the buyer's biggest fear (doesn't work → refund family; late → SLA; no results → performance/extension; is this real → comparison/specific proof), your refund tolerance, buyer sophistication (premium → anti-guarantee can signal confidence; first-time → generous refund; B2B → SLA/pilot), and outcome measurability (measurable → performance/extension; fuzzy → conditional refund gated on effort).

Rules: max two conditions (three only if tightly related); put it near the buy button in plain language; stress-test at a 10-20% invocation rate (`scripts/pricing_calc.py guarantee`); proof first, guarantee second; re-read twice a year as the offer changes. "Cancel anytime" is a term, not a guarantee.

Good wording patterns: specific ("Finish weeks 1-4; if you can't point to one concrete result, email us for a full refund"), confident ("If it doesn't work for you, we don't want your money"), candid ("Guarantees can feel salesy; here's ours anyway…"). Avoid "100% satisfaction guaranteed", "lifetime guarantee" with no terms, stacked guarantees, legalese.

Examples by type: course → completion-gated refund; coaching → exit after first two sessions with unused balance refunded; agency → paid first-month pilot with milestone gate; mastermind (premium) → no refunds after a rigorous application; template pack → 30-day no-questions; SaaS → free trial or 30-day refund on annual prepay; direct response → strong risk reversal.

## 4. Bonuses

Bonuses raise perceived value, lower risk ("even if the core disappoints, I got X") and, most importantly, close specific objections.

Find objections: refund requests, sales-call notes, cancel reasons, "what almost stopped you buying?" asked to 3-5 recent buyers, reading your page aloud and noting doubts. They cluster into 3-6.

| Objection | Bonus that closes it |
|---|---|
| No time to implement | Done-for-you setup in week 1 |
| Don't know which tools | Vetted tool stack with setup guides |
| What if I get stuck? | 30-60 days of async support / office hours |
| Team won't buy in | Stakeholder deck or ROI calculator |
| Tried before, failed | Case study from someone in the same situation |
| My industry is different | Industry-specific playbook |
| Won't finish | Accountability check-in at day 30 |
| Complex situation | 1:1 kickoff to tailor the plan |
| Switching is painful | Migration done for you |

Structure (3-5 bonuses): speed (templates, accelerators), trust (case studies, methodology doc), stuck (support), decision (pre-vetted choices), optional bigger-than-asked (adjacent outcome).

Value rules: stated values must be verifiable against something you or others actually sell; total bonus value under ~2× the core price; if you'd never sell it alone, don't put a dollar figure on it; name each with a specific outcome. Avoid bonuses that imply the core is incomplete, bonuses doing the core's job, padding to 8+, and table-stakes "bonuses" every competitor offers.

Timing: day 1 speed bonuses; weeks 2-4 support bonuses; completion bonuses (certificate, alumni access, advanced session) to encourage finishing and referrals.

Audit each bonus: which objection? defensible value? right timing? specific to this buyer? would the offer still sell on the bonus alone (if yes, fix the core)?

## 5. Scarcity and urgency

Scarcity limits quantity; urgency limits time. Only use constraints that are real, verifiable and enforced.

Honest formats: capacity (we onboard N clients per month), cohort (enrollment closes on date; next cohort later), founding-member pricing (first N customers, show the count), inventory/batch, seasonal (priced for the season start), bonus expiry (bonus removed after date, really removed), scheduled price increase (announced date, then raised).

Fake patterns to never use: timers that reset per visit, "only 3 spots" that never changes, invented demand, "last chance" that isn't, perpetual "limited time" bonuses. Screenshots of these spread and outlive the campaign.

When not to use scarcity: cancel-anytime subscriptions, low-price impulse buys (~$5-30), luxury brands (explicit scarcity reads low-status), buyers already decided.

Checklist: what's the real constraint; how to state it plainly; can the buyer verify it; will you enforce it; is it placed next to the CTA? Real scarcity on a strong offer speeds decisions; scarcity on a weak offer just speeds the "no".

## 6. Naming

Named offers are easier to repeat, distinct from generic services, and support higher prices.
- Outcome-named: "The 30-Day Activation Sprint"
- Method-named: "The Five-Angle Page Framework"
- Identity-named: "Founder Marketing Room"
- Structure-named: "5-Day Launch Cohort"

Avoid generic descriptors ("Marketing Coaching Program"), internal tier codes, hype names, and pun overload. Test: can a buyer text a friend "I joined the [name]: $X, and you get [one-line outcome]"?

## 7. Price and payment structure

Buyers compare price to the outcome, the next-best alternative, the cost of doing nothing, and your other offers. Change perception without changing the number: quantify the cost of inaction, anchor to a pricier alternative, sequence higher-priced items first.

| Structure | Works when | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Pay in full (often with a small discount) | High trust, lower prices | Smaller pool |
| 2-4 installments (total slightly higher) | Mid-high price, hesitant buyers | Defaults; collect via card on file |
| Subscription | Ongoing value | Churn |
| Pay after results | Confident, measurable delivery | Cash-flow lag |
| Deposit + balance on milestone | Services | Balance risk |
| Paid pilot credited to contract | B2B, new vendor risk | Pilot scoping effort |
| Trial → paid | SaaS, digital products | Drop-off at conversion |

Often the lever is a payment plan, not a lower price: "$6,000 today" and "3 × $2,000" convert very differently.

## 8. Formats by business type

| Type | Core | Bonuses | Guarantee | Scarcity | Price/payment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Service/freelance | Scoped engagement, named deliverable, timeline | Templates/frameworks for self-sufficiency after | Paid pilot or first-milestone gate | Capacity (next slot date) | Project with deposit or retainer |
| Course (async) | Curriculum + format | Templates, swipes, quick-start | Generous unconditional | Bonus expiry, scheduled increase | Often $297-$1,997; full or installments |
| Course (cohort) | Live program | Community + office hours + reviews | Completion-gated | Cohort start date, seat cap | Often $1,500-$5,000+ |
| Coaching 1:1 | Sessions + async access + outcome focus | Library, intros, check-ins | Exit after two sessions | 4-10 client capacity | Monthly or 3/6/12-month |
| Group/mastermind | The room + peer accountability | Library, events | Trial period then no refunds | 8-30 seats | Monthly or annual |
| Info product | Asset + defined "lifetime" access | Walkthrough, adjacent assets | 30-day no-questions | Bonus expiry, price increase | Often $29-$497, pay in full |
| High-ticket B2B | Annual contract or multi-month engagement | Onboarding, training, integration, CSM | SLA, pilot, performance terms | Quarter-end terms, onboarding capacity | Annual, quarterly billing, custom |
| Agency retainer | Ongoing deliverables with defined volume | Reporting, strategy sessions | 90-day milestone out | Client capacity | Monthly with minimum term |
| Direct response / ecommerce | Product bundle | Gifts, accessories, content | Strong risk reversal | Real stock or season | Bundles, subscribe-and-save |

Watch-outs: services compete on price until named and productized (fixed scope, timeline, price, bonuses, gate); scope creep kills service margins (write in/out/optional before starting); course completion is the marketing asset (engineer a week-1 win); coaching renewal is the real conversion event; info products are judged in the first five minutes (premium onboarding); define what "lifetime" means.

## 9. SaaS offers and the discount trap

Discounts to acquire new customers anchor the product as cheap, attract price-shoppers, train the market to wait for sales, and blend into discount fatigue. Discount only for upgrades and cross-sells (rewarding existing commitment) or real, time-bound seasonal windows everyone can see. `scripts/pricing_calc.py discount` shows how many more sales a discount needs to break even.

Constructed SaaS offers that work instead of discounts (illustrative patterns):
- **Paid pilot**: fixed-price 30-day pilot on the buyer's real data; refunded if no actionable insight, credited to annual if they convert; done-with-you setup; capacity-limited (N pilots/month).
- **Season bundle**: tools that only pay off together, bundled at a price valid until the season starts; "use it live, refund if it doesn't beat your current workflow" guarantee.
- **Accelerator**: plan + structured 6-week program to reach the first outcome; "reach the milestone in 6 weeks or we keep working with you free"; cohort start dates.
- **Switch offer**: done-for-you migration at a fixed fee; "live and verified in 14 days or it's free" (guarantee on the switching cost, the real objection); bonus audit to remove the second-biggest fear.

## 10. Ladders

- **Order bump** (checkout checkbox): small, related, ≤ ~30-50% of core price; take rates often 10-30%.
- **Post-purchase upsell**: one-click, complements the core (speed, done-for-you, more volume); offer a downsell (smaller version or payment plan) if declined.
- **Continuity**: membership, support plan, or software subscription that keeps the result going.
- **Annual upgrade**: offer annual after first value (e.g., day 30) with the saving in currency.
- **Expansion**: usage limit prompts that present the next tier as the natural next step (see conversion-optimization paywalls).
Use `scripts/funnel.py upsell` in conversion-optimization for AOV math.

## 11. Banned patterns in offer copy

Hype adjectives (game-changing, revolutionary, next-level, 10×), "secret/hidden/what they don't want you to know", "limited time" without a date, "$X value" without a comparable, "100% guaranteed" without conditions, gold-and-countdown styling on a serious product. Use specific numbers, named customers, concrete outcomes and real dates.

## 12. Worked before/after offers

| Business | Before | After | Lever moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fractional CMO | "Marketing leadership, $8k/month, 6-month minimum" | "90-Day Pipeline Reset": fixed scope (positioning, channel plan, hiring plan), $8k/month, month-1 milestone gate with exit, 2 openings per quarter | Likelihood (gate), name, honest capacity |
| Cohort copy course $1,997 | "Learn copywriting in 8 weeks" + unconditional refund | Named method, 8 look-alike case studies, week-1 first-win exercise, completion-gated refund, 3 objection bonuses, cohort cap 40 | Likelihood, time to first win |
| Template pack $97 | 60 templates, no guarantee | "First 90 Days System": 10-minute quick-start video, 30-day no-questions refund, launch-week bonus that truly expires | Effort, risk reversal |
| B2B SaaS $50k ACV | Annual contract, standard terms | Paid 60-day pilot credited to contract, success criteria agreed upfront, uptime SLA, strategic onboarding named package | Likelihood, risk |
| Group mastermind $4k | Generic 30-day refund, open enrollment | Application + interview, 20-seat cap, anti-guarantee framed as commitment, peer matching on day 1 | Identity, honest scarcity |
| Content agency retainer | "4 blog posts/month" | "Organic Pipeline Program": topic research + 4 posts + distribution + monthly pipeline report; 90-day milestone exit | Dream outcome, likelihood |

Pattern: fix the core and name it first, then add one risk-reversal and one honest urgency mechanism; bonuses only for real objections.
