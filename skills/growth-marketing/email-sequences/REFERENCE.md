# Email sequences reference

Contents: 1 principles · 2 sequence blueprints · 3 email-type catalog · 4 subject lines and preview text · 5 copy structure, length, CTAs · 6 personalization and segmentation · 7 testing · 8 metrics and benchmarks · 9 deliverability and compliance · 10 audit checklist.

## 1. Principles

- One email, one job, one primary CTA.
- Value before the ask: earn the right to sell with usefulness.
- Relevance over volume: fewer, better, segmented emails.
- Every email moves the reader somewhere; every link does something useful.
- Triggered by behavior wherever possible; time-based delays are the fallback.
- Exit on goal; suppress overlapping programs.

## 2. Sequence blueprints

**Welcome (after newsletter or account signup)**: 5-7 emails over 12-14 days. Goal: deliver value, build trust, convert.
1. Immediate: deliver what was promised, one next step, set expectations (what you'll send, how often).
2. Day 1-2: quick win ("get your first [result] in 10 minutes").
3. Day 3-4: why we exist / origin story tied to their problem.
4. Day 5-6: proof (customer story close to their situation).
5. Day 7-8: handle the top objection ("I don't have time for X").
6. Day 9-11: highlight an underused capability with a direct CTA to try it.
7. Day 12-14: the offer: summarize value, clear offer, honest urgency, risk reversal.

**Lead nurture (after lead magnet)**: 6-8 emails over 2-3 weeks.
1. Immediate: deliver + brief intro + preview of what's coming.
2. Day 2-3: expand on the magnet's topic (the next step it didn't cover).
3. Day 4-5: deep-dive on the problem and its cost.
4. Day 6-8: a framework or method for solving it.
5. Day 9-11: case study.
6. Day 12-14: how we're different (vs alternatives, including doing nothing).
7. Day 15-18: objection handler.
8. Day 19-21: direct offer (demo, trial, purchase).

**Product onboarding (signup → activation)**: 5-7 emails over 14 days; coordinate with in-app guidance.
1. Immediate: welcome + the single first step toward activation.
2. Day 1: getting-started help (short video, template, sample data) if the step isn't done.
3. Day 2-3: key feature that drives the aha moment.
4. Day 4-5: success story from a similar user.
5. Day 7: check-in; offer human help (reply-to a person).
6. Day 10-12: advanced tip for activated users.
7. Day 14+: upgrade or next milestone.

**New paying customer**: 3-5 emails over 14 days. Reinforce the decision, drive full adoption, prevent early churn: thank you + what's next (immediate); setup checklist for full value (day 2); pro tips for paid features (day 5); story from a similar customer (day 7); check-in + support resources (day 14). Focus on reinforcement and expansion, not conversion.

**Trial-to-paid**: 3-5 emails, triggered by time (days 10, 12, 14 of a 14-day trial) or behavior (hit a limit, used a premium feature, highly active). Structure: value summary (what they accomplished) → what they lose or gain on paid → proof (who upgraded and why) → deadline (trial end, honest) → last chance with an easy path.

**Re-engagement**: 3-4 emails over ~2 weeks after 30-60 days of inactivity: genuine check-in → what's new/value reminder → incentive or help offer → last call (stay subscribed or be removed). Remove non-responders (sunset) to protect deliverability.

**Win-back (expired trials)**: 3-4 over 30 days: day 1 trial ended + what you'd keep → day 7 what held you back (one-question survey) → day 14 offer (extended trial, setup call; discount sparingly) → day 30 door's open. Segment by trial engagement: high (remove friction to convert), low (fresh start with more help), none (ask what happened, offer a demo).

**Win-back (cancelled customers)**: 2-3 over 90 days: day 30 what's new → day 60 "we fixed [their cancel reason]" → day 90 return offer. Personalize by cancellation reason; no guilt.

**Failed payment (dunning)**: 3-4 over 7-14 days, alongside automatic card retries: day 0 friendly notice + update link → day 3 reminder, service may pause → day 7 urgent, account will be suspended on [date] → day 10-14 final notice, what they'll lose. Assume an accident; one CTA; no guilt. Add pre-dunning: card-expiring emails 30 and 7 days before expiry. (More in retention-and-referrals.)

## 3. Email-type catalog

| Category | Email | Trigger | Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onboarding | Setup-step reminder | Critical step undone after X (integration 48h, teammate invite 3 days, profile 24h) | Remind what they started, why the step matters, direct link to do it, offer help; 1-3 emails |
| Onboarding | Teammate invite | User invites someone | From the inviter's name, what they're joining, single accept CTA; reminders day 2 and 5 |
| Retention | Upgrade tier | 80% of seats, 90% of usage, tried a higher-tier feature, power-user pattern | Celebrate growth, show what the next tier adds, value vs cost, one-click upgrade |
| Retention | Review request | 30/60/90-day milestone, achieved result, great support resolution, renewal; never after bugs or billing issues | Thank, cite their milestone, why reviews help others, direct link, short |
| Retention | Proactive support | Usage drop, repeated failures, repeated help-doc visits, stuck step | Genuine concern, specific ("I noticed…") if appropriate, direct help from a named person, no pitch |
| Retention | Usage report | Weekly/monthly/quarterly | Key activity, comparison with last period, milestones, one suggestion, light CTA ("You saved 6 hours this month") |
| Retention | NPS survey | Quarterly or post-milestone | One question first; personal sender; say how feedback is used. Follow up: 9-10 thank + review/referral ask; 7-8 "what would make it a 10?"; 0-6 personal outreach |
| Retention | Referral ask | Promoter score, achieved result, renewal, campaign | Remind their success, clear two-sided offer, unique link |
| Billing | Switch to annual | Around renewal, after 3-6 months, year end, before a price increase | Exact savings in currency, lock current price, one-click switch |
| Billing | Renewal reminder | 30/7 days before annual renewal (required in some jurisdictions) | Amount, date, what's included, how to change plan |
| Billing | Cancellation survey | On cancel | One required reason + optional detail; route to save offers (see retention-and-referrals) |
| Usage | Milestones and key events | Thresholds reached (100th invoice) | Celebrate, show the next milestone, optional share/referral |
| Campaign | Newsletter/roundup | Fixed day monthly or weekly | Consistent day/time, scannable, mix of product tips, customer stories, education, news; one featured CTA |
| Campaign | Seasonal promotion | Calendar moments (year-end, sales events) | 2-4 email burst with a real deadline; segment out recent buyers |
| Campaign | Product update | Major release | Benefit first, short demo, who it's for, one CTA to try |
| Campaign | Pricing update | Price change | Plain facts, effective date, why, soft landing (see pricing-and-offers) |

## 4. Subject lines and preview text

Subject: clear > clever, specific > vague, benefit or genuine curiosity; ~30-60 characters (mobile shows ~35-45); test emoji, they polarize; personalization only when it adds meaning.

Patterns: question ("Still chasing receipts on the 28th?"); how-to ("How to close the month in 2 days"); number ("3 approval rules that stop surprise spend"); direct ("Maya, your cards are ready"); story tease ("The expense policy mistake we made"); outcome + time ("Your first matched receipt in 2 minutes").

Preview text: 40-140 characters, extends or completes the subject, never "View in browser" or a repeat.

Avoid: fake "Re:" or "Fwd:", "You won't believe…", all caps, "!!!", "urgent", "free!!!", misleading urgency. The subject must hold up if it's the only line they read.

## 5. Copy structure, length, CTAs

Structure: hook (open with the reason you're writing) → context (why it matters to them) → value → CTA → human sign-off with a real name.

Formatting: 1-3 sentence paragraphs; white space; bullets for scannable steps; bold sparingly; mobile-first (most opens are on phones); alt text on images; plain-text-style emails often outperform heavy designs for B2B and founder-voice programs.

Tone: conversational; I/we and you; active voice; read aloud.

Email-specific tells to cut: "I hope this email finds you well", "Just checking in", "Great news!", "Feel free to reach out if you have any questions", em dashes in subject/preview (1-2 max in body).

Length: transactional 50-125 words; educational 150-300; story-driven 300-500. Long only when it earns it.

CTAs: one primary per email; button for primary action, inline link for secondary; button text = action + outcome ("Match my first receipt"); repeat the primary CTA once in long emails.

## 6. Personalization and segmentation

Merge fields: first name (fallback "there"), company, plan, real usage data. Dynamic blocks by segment, behavior or stage. Triggered emails (action, milestone, inactivity) beat time-based for relevance.

Segments: behavior (clickers vs non-clickers, active vs inactive), stage (trial vs paid, new vs tenured, engaged vs at-risk), profile (industry, role, use case, company size), source (which lead magnet or campaign).

## 7. Testing

Test one variable at a time with enough recipients (see analytics-and-experiments for sample sizes; small lists should test big differences only). Order of impact: subject lines → offer/CTA → send timing and cadence → length → personalization depth → design. Document every result in an experiment log. For sequences, test whole-sequence variants against a holdout on the goal metric.

## 8. Metrics and benchmarks

| Metric | Typical range (directional) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate | 20-40% reported | Inflated by mail privacy proxies; use only for relative comparisons within the same audience |
| Click rate (of delivered) | 1-5% | Primary engagement signal |
| Click-to-open | 10-20% | Content relevance, if opens are usable |
| Unsubscribe per send | < 0.5% | Higher = relevance or frequency issue |
| Spam complaints | < 0.1% | Major inbox providers enforce ~0.3% as a hard ceiling for bulk senders |
| Bounce rate | < 2% | Higher = list hygiene or capture problem |
| Goal conversion | sequence-specific | The metric that matters |
| Revenue per recipient | | For ecommerce and paid offers |

## 9. Deliverability and compliance

- Authenticate sending domains: SPF, DKIM, DMARC (start p=none, move to quarantine/reject); align the From domain; use a subdomain for marketing if transactional mail must be protected.
- Bulk-sender rules (Gmail and Yahoo since Feb 2024, Outlook.com since May 2025; apply to senders of ~5,000+ messages a day to that provider): SPF, DKIM and DMARC with an aligned From domain, one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 `List-Unsubscribe` + `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers) honored within 2 days, user-reported spam rate under 0.1% and never 0.3% or higher. Gmail moved from warnings to temporary and permanent rejections of non-compliant mail from November 2025.
- Warm up new domains/IPs gradually; send to the most engaged first.
- List hygiene: confirmed opt-in where quality is poor, remove hard bounces immediately, sunset contacts with no clicks in 90-180 days after a re-engagement attempt, never buy lists.
- Consent and law: honor opt-outs promptly; include a physical address; record consent source and time; follow regional rules (opt-in consent in the EU/UK and Canada; CAN-SPAM in the US; India's DPDP Act 2023 needs free, specific, informed consent with easy withdrawal; transactional vs marketing distinctions).
- Content: balanced text/image ratio, no link shorteners, consistent From name, avoid attachments in marketing mail.

## 10. Audit checklist

- Onboarding: welcome immediate? activation-focused? setup reminders? teammate invites?
- Retention: upgrade prompts at limits? usage reports? review and referral asks at the right moments? NPS follow-ups?
- Billing: annual switch? renewal reminders? dunning + card-expiry emails? cancellation survey?
- Usage: milestone emails?
- Win-back: expired trial and cancelled-customer sequences, segmented?
- Campaigns: newsletter with consistent cadence? promotions with real deadlines? product and pricing updates?
- Hygiene: exits and suppressions, authentication, complaint and bounce rates, sunset policy.
