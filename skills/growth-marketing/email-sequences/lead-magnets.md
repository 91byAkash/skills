# Lead magnets reference

Contents: 1 principles · 2 formats and build guidance · 3 matching to buyer stage · 4 gating and form fields · 5 landing page, delivery, thank-you page · 6 promotion · 7 benchmarks · 8 lead quality · 9 cost and time · 10 tests.

## 1. Principles

1. Solve one specific problem ("cold emails that get replies" beats "marketing guide").
2. Match the buyer stage (education for awareness, evaluation help for consideration, implementation help for decision).
3. High perceived value, low time cost: looks worth paying for; consumable in under 30 minutes (ideally under 10); one immediate takeaway.
4. Natural path to the product: solves a problem the product also solves, or exposes a gap the product fills.
5. One format, works on mobile, no special software.

Before designing, find: current capture methods and conversion rate, existing assets to repurpose (posts, internal templates, data), expertise to package, goal (list growth, lead quality, product education), stage targeted, and resources.

## 2. Formats and build guidance

| Format | Best for | Effort | Build notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checklist | Processes, audits, launches | 1-2 hours | 10-25 items grouped in phases; each item actionable and checkable; one page; brand lightly |
| Cheat sheet | Reference, shortcuts, decision matrices | 2-4 hours | Dense but scannable; tables; one page or two; printable |
| Template (sheet, doc, workspace) | Repeatable workflows, planning, tracking | 2-8 hours | Usable within 5 minutes: example rows filled in, instructions tab, locked formulas, copy-to-your-drive link |
| Swipe file | Examples and inspiration (subject lines, pages, ads) | 4-8 hours | 20-100 annotated examples, organized by type/use; say why each works |
| Guide/ebook | Authority, deep education | 1-3 weeks | 10-25 pages; problem → framework → steps → examples → next step; skimmable headers; avoid fluff padding |
| Email mini-course | Education + nurture | 1-2 weeks | 5-7 lessons, one per 1-2 days; each lesson stands alone with one action; final lesson bridges to the product |
| Video mini-course | Education + personality | 2-4 weeks | 3-5 lessons of 5-10 minutes; each self-contained |
| Quiz/assessment | Segmentation, engagement | 1-2 weeks | 5-10 questions; 3-5 result types mapped to segments and follow-up sequences; quiz ungated, personalized results behind the email |
| Calculator/tool | High-intent, link-earning | 1-4 weeks | Instant result on page; gate the detailed report, not the basic answer |
| Webinar/workshop | Authority, live engagement | ~1 week prep | Teach something useful (not a disguised pitch); 30-45 min + Q&A; replay + summary + CTA within 24h; evergreen versions for scale |
| Resource library | Return visits | Ongoing | Curated, updated; gate the full library or not at all |
| Free trial / community access | Product experience | varies | The product itself as the magnet |

## 3. Matching to buyer stage

| Stage | Goal | Formats | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Educate on the problem, attract strangers | Checklist, cheat sheet, guide, quiz | "Month-end close checklist for agencies" |
| Consideration | Help evaluate solutions | Comparison template, assessment, case-study collection, webinar | "Expense tool comparison sheet (12 criteria)" |
| Decision | Help implement, remove purchase friction | Templates, trial, migration guide, ROI calculator | "Switch in 30 minutes: migration checklist", "Calculate your hours saved" |

## 4. Gating and form fields

| Approach | When | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Full gate | High-value, bottom-funnel assets | Max capture, less reach and fewer links |
| Partial gate | Preview free, full version gated | Balance of reach and capture |
| Ungated + optional signup | Top-funnel education, link-earning assets | Max reach, low capture |
| Content upgrade | Post-specific bonus inside a blog post | Contextual, high intent; often 2-5× better than generic sidebar CTAs |

Fields: email only (highest conversion) → email + name (personalization) → email + company/role (qualification) → multi-field only for webinars/demos. Each extra field typically costs ~5-10% of conversions; use enrichment instead of asking where possible. Use progressive profiling for repeat downloaders.

Framing: make the value concrete ("the 25-page guide"), show a preview (table of contents, page images, sample result), social proof ("downloaded by 5,000+ finance leads"), reduce risk ("no spam, unsubscribe anytime").

## 5. Landing page, delivery, thank-you page

Landing page order: benefit headline (what they get and why it matters) → mockup/preview → 3-5 bullets of what's inside → proof (count, testimonial, logos) → short form with specific CTA ("Send me the checklist") → FAQ (Is it free? What format? Will I get spam?).

| Delivery | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Instant download | Immediate gratification | No email verification |
| Email delivery | Verifies the address, starts the relationship | Small delay, spam-folder risk |
| Thank-you page + email copy | Instant access and verified email | Slightly more setup |
| Drip delivery | Habit and multiple touchpoints | Only for courses/series |

Thank-you page: confirm delivery ("check your inbox"), one next step (book a call, start a trial, join the community), optional pre-written share, related content. Then start the nurture sequence (REFERENCE.md §2).

## 6. Promotion

- Blog: inline and end-of-post CTAs; post-specific content upgrades.
- Popups: exit intent or scroll depth, offer matched to page topic (see conversion-optimization).
- Social: snippets, carousels of key points, magnet as the profile link (see content-strategy social.md).
- Paid: social lead forms for top-funnel magnets, search ads for high-intent templates/tools, B2B network ads for professional assets, retargeting blog visitors (see paid-ads).
- Partners: cross-promotion, guest webinars, partner newsletters, resource bundles.

## 7. What to expect (rough ranges; traffic quality and the offer move them a lot)

Per format, landing-page sign-up rate and the kind of list it tends to build:

| Format | Sign-up rate on its own page | Typical list it builds |
|---|---|---|
| Free trial | 5-15% | Small and very close to buying; the most valuable per lead |
| Specific template | 25-45% | Mid-sized, practical, strong buying intent |
| Webinar | 20-40% register, and 30-50% of those attend live | Smaller, engaged, high intent |
| Industry report | no reliable benchmark; track your own | Mid-sized, decent intent, good for links |
| Quiz / assessment | 30-50% of people who finish | Large, and the answers let you segment it |
| Checklist | 30-50% | Large and easy to get; intent is often shallow |
| Cheat sheet | 25-40% | Large; similar to a checklist |
| Guide / ebook | 20-35% | Large when generic, with modest intent; better when narrow |
| Mini-course | 15-30% | Smaller, committed readers |

Where the offer appears matters as much as what it is. Inside a relevant blog post, expect roughly 3-8% of that post's readers; a dedicated page converts around 20-40% of warm or organic visitors and 10-25% of paid cold traffic (as low as 5-15% when very cold); a link from a social profile around 10-20%; exit-intent popups 2-5% of visitors; sidebars and banners 0.5-2%. Across industries, landing pages mostly land between 10% and 35%. For B2B, 1-5% of leads becoming customers is common, and magnet subscribers typically open 30-50% of nurture emails and click 2-5% (directional).

## 8. Lead quality

Signs of a good lead: opens and engages with the first three emails, clicks through to product or pricing, comes back within 30 days, matches the ICP, and starts a trial, demo or purchase within 90 days. Signs of a poor one: unsubscribes early, never opens anything after delivery, uses a throwaway address, sits outside the ICP, or only ever wants free content.

Broad formats (generic ebooks, broad checklists) grow the list fastest but convert least; narrow, job-specific formats (templates, trials, webinars, reports on the buyer's own industry) grow it more slowly and convert far better. Judge each magnet by lead-to-customer rate and revenue, not list growth, and retire any magnet that adds subscribers who never buy.

## 9. Cost and time

Directional cost per lead by channel: organic search, content upgrades and partner co-promotion are close to free ($0-5); paid social runs about $3-15 (cheaper for consumers, dearer for B2B); paid search about $10-50; B2B professional-network ads about $25-75.

Time to build: a checklist or cheat sheet in a day; a template or swipe file in one to two days; a quiz or email course in one to two weeks; a guide in one to three weeks; a video course in two to four weeks. Hiring help costs anything from a few hundred dollars for designing a checklist to several thousand for producing a video course.

## 10. Tests

Headline (benefit vs curiosity), format (checklist vs guide on the same topic), gate level (full vs partial), fields (email only vs email + name), CTA text ("Download the guide" vs "Send me the checklist"), delivery (instant vs email), preview image vs none.
