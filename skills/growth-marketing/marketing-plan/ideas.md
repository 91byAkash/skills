# Marketing idea library

Read this when the user asks for marketing ideas, when filling the idea bank of a marketing plan, or when a launch needs extra channels. Pick 3-5 ideas that fit stage, budget, team and audience; depth beats breadth. Rank candidates with `ice` in [scripts/plan_tool.py](scripts/plan_tool.py).

## 1. How to choose

**Ask first** (skip what `.agents/product-marketing.md` already answers): stage and main goal; budget and team size; what has been tried and what happened; which rival tactics they admire; what the brand would never do.

**By stage**
- Pre-launch: waitlist with referral priority, early-access pricing, launch-platform preparation, build-in-public posts.
- Early (first customers): low-competition search terms, founder-led selling and social, one community, founder welcome emails, comparison pages.
- Growth: paid search and social on proven messages, partnerships and integrations, events and webinars, referral program.
- Scale: brand campaigns, international expansion and price localisation, media acquisitions, own conference.

**By budget**: free (content and search, community participation, social, thoughtful commenting, newsletter swaps, permissionless templates) → low (targeted ads, newsletter sponsorships, free tools, review-site presence) → medium (events, partnership programs, PR help, video) → high (acquisitions, conference sponsorship, brand and out-of-home campaigns).

**By time to result**: quick (search ads, email to existing list, social, launch platforms) → medium (content, search, community, partnerships) → long (brand, thought leadership, platform and network effects).

**By goal**
- Leads fast: search ads on high-intent terms, B2B social ads by job title, a free tool that captures email.
- Authority: conference talks, a book or definitive guide, a podcast or podcast tour, original research.
- Low budget: low-competition keywords, genuine community participation, comment marketing, newsletter swaps.
- Product-led: viral loops, "made with" badges, in-app upgrade prompts, templates.
- Enterprise: investor and advisor introductions, certified-expert networks, targeted conference presence, account-based direct mail.

**Score each candidate** on impact, confidence and ease (1-10 each); multiply; fund in rank order within budget. Anything with confidence ≤ 3 runs as a small test with a kill rule before real spend.

**Write each recommendation as**: idea and one-line description; why it fits this company; first 2-3 steps; what success looks like (metric and time frame); resources (hours, money, skills).

## 2. The library

### Content and search
| Idea | What it is |
|---|---|
| Low-competition keywords | Target long-tail and emerging queries rivals ignore; quickest organic wins for a new domain |
| Public self-audit | Audit your own site or process publicly and document fixes; builds credibility |
| Glossary | One page per industry term answering "what is X" |
| Programmatic pages | Template-driven pages for repeatable patterns (integrations, locations, comparisons, use cases); *needs unique data per page or it reads as thin* |
| Repurposing | One cornerstone piece becomes a thread, video, episode, carousel and email |
| Original data | Reports from your own product data or surveys; earns links and citations |
| Internal linking | Topic clusters that pass authority and help crawling |
| Content refresh | Update winning pages with new data and examples; often beats new content |
| Help-center search | Optimise support articles for problem-solution queries |
| Third-party publishing | Publish on high-authority platforms that rank faster than a new domain; *canonical back where possible* |

## 3. Stunts, guerrilla and direct mail

The goal is a memorable, low-cost break-through that can later become a repeatable program. Two rules: design a story the recipient wants to retell, and test small before spending big.

**Direct mail three-part test** (fail any one → redesign):
1. **Relevant**: the item connects to the product, the message or something the recipient cares about. A logo mug is noise; an object that lands a joke about their exact problem is memorable.
2. **A gift, not a pitch**: it opens or deepens a relationship. If it feels like a sales letter, you paid to annoy someone.
3. **Precisely targeted**: a short, hand-qualified list of named people where one deal pays for dozens of packages.

**Economics**: a $50-200 package to an unqualified lead is waste. Reserve it for named accounts and hard-to-reach decision-makers, send to a handful first, track replies and meetings, and scale only the version that earns responses.

**Patterns worth borrowing** (borrow the pattern, not the prop):
- An oversized, absurd object mailed to a short list of executives who ignore email, with a note tying the absurdity to the pitch.
- A physical jab at a rival staged at the rival's own event (for example, cash frozen in a block of ice with a sign about frozen accounts).
- Visibility above or around a competitor-heavy event without buying a booth.
- Food delivered to a few hundred target accounts with a personal note; one infrastructure startup reported roughly $15k spent, about 75 customers and seven figures of revenue from a campaign like this.
- One-of-a-kind memos written for a single prospect.
- Collectible cards or characters featuring the team and community.
- A branded reaction-image library that rides along in other people's conversations.
- An entertaining branded film made as entertainment first.
- A sponsored extreme feat so large it becomes the news (big-budget brands only).

**Running one**: pick the story → qualify the list → run the three-part test → ship to a handful → measure replies, meetings or coverage → turn the winner into a recurring program.

*Check postal, gift and anti-bribery rules (many companies cap gifts to employees, and public-sector buyers often cannot accept any).*
